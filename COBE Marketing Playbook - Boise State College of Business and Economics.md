# COBE Marketing Playbook - Boise State College of Business and Economics

## 1. Quick Start Guide
**Purpose:** A one-stop manual to plan and run COBE marketing.

**Who should use this:** Program leads, staff, student workers, interns, and faculty supporting outreach.

**5 steps to run a simple campaign**
1. **Define the goal** – What result is needed (inquiries, RSVPs, applications)? → See [Section 6](#6-campaign-frameworks-and-calendar).
2. **Pick the audience** – Who must respond? → See [Section 4](#4-audience-overview).
3. **Choose channels** – Website, email, social, events. → See [Section 5](#5-channel-playbooks).
4. **Build content** – Draft copy, visuals, CTAs, approvals. → See [Section 8](#8-content-templates-and-examples).
5. **Measure and learn** – Track KPIs, report, and adjust. → See [Section 9](#9-measurement-kpis-and-scorecard).

## 2. Purpose and Context
- **What COBE is:** College of Business and Economics at Boise State serving undergraduate and graduate students, working professionals, employers/partners, and alumni.
- **Link to RHM playbook:** This document adapts the Resort & Hospitality Management (RHM) handbook to a COBE-wide guide. RHM-specific examples are labeled **RHM example**; COBE-wide rules are labeled **COBE standard**.
- **What good marketing looks like at COBE:** Clear goals, consistent brand voice, lead times that respect channel requirements, measurable outcomes, and repeatable processes that help programs partner with the central COBE Marketing team.

## 3. Brand Foundations (COBE standard unless noted)
### Values & Personality
Practical, student-centered, career-focused, welcoming, and collaborative with industry.

### Positioning Statements
- COBE helps students and professionals turn business knowledge into real-world career momentum.
- COBE connects learners and industry to build the region’s next generation of leaders.

### Tone of Voice
- **Do:** Be direct, welcoming, and action-oriented; highlight outcomes and industry links; keep copy concise.
- **Do Not:** Overuse jargon; write in third person when a second-person call to action is clearer; bury next steps.

### Message Pillars
1. **Career Outcomes (COBE standard):** Programs lead to internships, jobs, and promotions.
   - “Build skills Boise employers hire for.”
   - “Graduate with a portfolio and network.”
2. **Applied Learning (COBE standard):** Projects, labs, and employer partnerships.
   - “Solve real business problems with industry mentors.”
   - “Hands-on courses that mirror workplace expectations.”
3. **Flexibility & Access (COBE standard):** On-campus, online, and certificate paths.
   - “Stackable options that fit work and life.”
   - “Start with a certificate, level up to a degree.”
4. **Community & Support (COBE standard):** Advising, career services, inclusive culture.
   - “You get coaches, not just classes.”
   - “Small cohorts; big network.”
5. **Industry Partnership (RHM example for illustration):** Hospitality trainings built with employers for front-line to management development.
   - “Free/low-cost micro-trainings when partners share workforce data.” (RHM example)
   - “Add one in-person leadership workshop to online modules.” (RHM example)

## 4. Audience Overview (COBE standard)
For each audience: who they are, what they care about, objections, and what COBE can promise.

- **Prospective undergraduates:** High-school seniors and transfers exploring majors. Care about cost, career paths, campus life. Objections: time, uncertainty. Promise: clear pathways, internships, supportive advising.
- **Prospective graduate students:** Early-career or pivoting professionals. Care about ROI, flexibility. Objections: balancing work/school. Promise: hybrid/online options, career acceleration, network.
- **Working professionals & certificate seekers:** Need quick upskilling. Care about immediate application and employer recognition. Promise: short, stackable modules; employer-valued badges.
- **Transfer students:** Want fast credit evaluation, no lost time. Promise: guided transfer maps and advisors.
- **Parents/influencers:** Want safety, affordability, outcomes. Promise: strong career placement and campus support.
- **Employers & partners:** Need talent and training. Promise: pipelines via fairs, mixers, and custom modules.
- **Alumni:** Want to give back and network. Promise: mentor and recruit; stay informed.

## 5. Channel Playbooks
### Website (COBE standard)
- **Role:** Primary source of truth for programs; capture inquiries and applications.
- **Program/Certificate Page Structure:**
  1. Hero: program name, one-line value, CTA button.
  2. Overview & who it’s for.
  3. Outcomes & careers.
  4. Curriculum snapshot & format (online/on-campus/hybrid).
  5. Faculty or industry connections.
  6. Testimonials/quotes.
  7. How to apply + deadlines.
  8. Contact/inquiry form.
- **Do:** Use plain language, scannable bullets, strong CTAs. **Do Not:** Post PDFs instead of pages; skip accessibility.

### Social Media (COBE standard)
- **Core platforms:** Instagram (student life), LinkedIn (careers/partners).
- **Posting cadence:** IG 3–4x/week; LinkedIn 2–3x/week.
- **Content mix:** Student stories, career outcomes, events, faculty highlights, tips/how-tos, behind the scenes.
- **Sample Posts (adapted from RHM patterns):**
  1. “Meet Jordan, a finance major who turned a COBE internship into a full-time offer. Ready to build your own story? Apply for spring internships by Oct 15.”
  2. “New certificate spotlight: Data Analytics for Business. 8-week online, built with local employers. Start now → [link]”
  3. “See you at the COBE Career Mixer tomorrow! Bring your resume—employers from tech, finance, and hospitality are hiring.”

### Email (COBE standard)
- **Campaign types:** Prospect nurture, event promotion, employer outreach, alumni updates.
- **Simple nurture flow (4–6 emails):**
  1. Welcome + value.
  2. Outcomes + testimonials.
  3. Program details + deadlines.
  4. How to get help (advising/Q&A).
  5. Final reminder + apply/register CTA.
- **Templates (adapted from RHM):**
  - **Subject patterns:** “Ready for your next role?,” “Seats open for [Program] this term,” “Join us at [Event] on [Date].”
  - **Body structure:** Hook → 2–3 bullets on value → proof (quote/stat) → CTA button/link → contact line.
  - **Example CTA:** “Start your application,” “Reserve your seat,” “Schedule a consult.”

### Events & Partnerships (COBE standard with RHM examples)
- **Promotion mix:**
  - Email to lists (2–3 sends: save-the-date, reminder, last call).
  - Social posts (announcement, countdown, day-of, recap).
  - Website event listing; add to COBE events page.
  - Campus screens/posters as needed (see lead times below).
- **Checklist (adapted from RHM event processes):**
  1. Lock date/venue; submit web/event listing **2+ weeks** ahead.
  2. Create digital display (1920x1080) and submit **3–4 weeks** prior if you want 1–2 weeks of run time. *(RHM example lead time)*
  3. Email request with subject “STUDENT ORG REQUEST” to cobe-info@boisestate.edu for web, digital screens, news, or posters. Include start/end dates, file names, and FDCC for printing if needed. *(RHM example process)*
  4. Post on COBE social; tag partners.
  5. Collect RSVPs; prep check-in list.
  6. Day-of: photos/quotes.
  7. Post-event: recap post and thank-you email; capture attendance.

## 6. Campaign Frameworks and Calendar
### Reusable Campaign Blueprint (COBE standard)
- Goal
- Target audience
- Main message & offer
- Channels (website, email, social, events)
- Key dates and lead times
- Content pieces (ads, posts, emails, page updates)
- Success metrics (inquiries, RSVPs, applications, clicks)

### Example Campaign Frameworks
1. **Program Launch/Refresh:** Goal: 30 inquiries. Audience: prospects & transfers. Message: “New 8-week online option.” Channels: webpage update, 3-email nurture, 5 social posts, campus screens. Metrics: inquiries, email CTR, page views.
2. **Internship/Employer Partnership Push (RHM example elements):** Goal: 10 employer signups for mixers or trainings. Message: “Add one in-person leadership workshop to your online staff modules.” Channels: LinkedIn posts, employer email, COBE news. Metrics: employer responses, meetings set.
3. **Enrollment/Deadline Campaign:** Goal: hit seat targets before deadline. Message: “Last day to apply for spring.” Channels: homepage banner, countdown emails, daily social reminders, classroom slides. Metrics: applications submitted, form completions.

## 7. Processes and Workflows (SOPs)
Each SOP is COBE standard unless noted.

### Request Marketing Support
- **Purpose:** Get help from COBE Marketing.
- **Who:** Program lead (requester), COBE Marketing.
- **Inputs:** Goal, audience, dates, draft content/assets.
- **Steps:**
  1. Email cobe-info@boisestate.edu with subject “[PROGRAM] Marketing Request.”
  2. Provide goal, audience, CTA, desired channels, and deadlines.
  3. Attach assets (size specs: 1920x1080 screens; posters 24x31) or ask for design help.
  4. Confirm approvals and accessibility needs.
- **Output:** Confirmed plan and schedule.

### Propose & Launch a Campaign
- **Purpose:** Align resources and timing.
- **Who:** Program lead (R), COBE Marketing (A), Faculty (C for content), Leadership (I for major pushes).
- **Steps:**
  1. Draft campaign blueprint (Section 6).
  2. Schedule channels; respect 2+ week lead for web/screens. *(RHM example lead time)*
  3. Build landing page/update existing page.
  4. Draft emails and social posts; route for review.
  5. Launch; monitor daily during final week.
- **Output:** Campaign live with tracking links.

### Gather Stories/Testimonials
- **Purpose:** Capture proof for outcomes.
- **Who:** Student worker or staff (R), Program lead (A), Faculty/Alumni/Employers (sources).
- **Steps:**
  1. Identify 3–5 candidates per term.
  2. Send short Q&A form; request photo/video permission.
  3. Draft 2–3 pull quotes and a 50–100 word story.
  4. Store assets in shared drive; note usage rights.
- **Output:** Approved quotes, photos, and posts.

### Submit Content for Review/Approval
- **Purpose:** Keep brand and accessibility aligned.
- **Who:** Content creator (R), COBE Marketing (A), Program lead (C), Leadership (I when sensitive).
- **Steps:**
  1. Send draft with channel, run dates, and sizes (e.g., 1920x1080 screens; posters 24x31; IG 1080x1080/1920x1080). *(RHM example sizes)*
  2. Include alt text and caption suggestions.
  3. Allow 2 business days for review when possible.
  4. Incorporate edits; confirm publish time.
- **Output:** Approved content ready to post.

### Close Out a Campaign & Capture Learnings
- **Purpose:** Improve next run.
- **Who:** Program lead (R/A), COBE Marketing (C), Stakeholders (I).
- **Steps:**
  1. Pull metrics (Section 9 scorecard).
  2. Compare to goals; note what worked/what lagged.
  3. Archive assets and links.
  4. Send a 1-page recap with recommendations.
- **Output:** Recap + updated playbook notes.

1. **Student Story:** “Meet [Name], a [major/program] who [outcome]. Next: [CTA].”
2. **Event Promo:** “[Event] on [Date]. Why attend: [3 bullets]. Save your spot → [link].”

### Email Templates
1. **Prospect Nurture:**
   - Subject: “Ready for your next role?”
   - Hook line; 2–3 bullets on value; proof quote; CTA button; contact line.
2. **Event Promotion:**
   - Subject: “Join us at [Event] on [Date]”

### Landing/Program Page Outline

## 9. Measurement, KPIs, and Scorecard
- **Core KPIs:** Reach/impressions, engagement rate, clicks/CTR, inquiries/forms, applications/enrollments, event RSVPs/attendance.
- **Channel Metrics:**
  - Website: page views, time on page, form completions.
  - Email: opens, clicks, replies, unsubscribes.
  - Social: impressions, saves/shares, link clicks.
  - Events: RSVPs, check-ins, follow-up actions.
- **Scorecard (monthly/quarterly):**
  - Rows: KPI per channel; Columns: Month 1, Month 2, Month 3; Notes column for what worked/changes.
- **How to read:** Rising clicks but flat inquiries → landing page needs clearer CTA. High opens/low clicks → sharpen subject-to-body match. Strong RSVPs/weak attendance → send day-before reminder.

## 10. Governance and Ownership
- **Ownership:**
  - COBE Marketing: brand standards, web/news/social governance, approvals for paid/major campaigns.
  - Program Leads: goals, content inputs, subject expertise, initial drafts.
  - Student Workers/Interns: execution support (posts, edits, data pulls) under supervision.
  - University Marketing: consult on major brand/paid media; ensure accessibility.
- **Rules:**
  - Use COBE voice and templates; get approval for new visuals/messages.
  - Publishing rights: COBE Marketing controls college channels; programs can post to program-owned social with oversight.
  - Loop leadership for high-visibility or sensitive topics.
